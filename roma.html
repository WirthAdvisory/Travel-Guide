Guide to Roma — for friends

Roma

Our favorite city in the world. Wine bars, carbonara arguments, churches you can wander into for free, and ruins that never stop being ruins.

Wine⌄

Vinoteca Novecento

Piazza delle Coppelle, 47 — 5 min walk from the Pantheon

Excellent little wine bar. Say hi to Giovanni from Ross & Lisa of Chicago!

Map ↗

Vinaietto – Old Winery

Lovely wine bar, found after a Colosseum + Forum tour. Good spot to unwind post-tour.

Map ↗
Restaurants⌄

LUCIANO Cucina Italiana

Amazing carbonara and cacio e pepe — our favorite in Roma for those two pasta dishes. Highly recommended; reservations recommended.

Map ↗

Fiaschetteria Beltramme

Via della Croce, 39 — 5 min walk from the Spanish Steps

Get the cacio e pepe, you will not regret it. Tiny place — reservations recommended (a credit card concierge can help book).

Map ↗

La Carbonara

Piazza Campo de' Fiori, 23

The penne alla carbonara is divine. The off-menu penne all'arrabbiata is also delightful. Reservation not a bad idea, but you can usually walk up.

Map ↗

Osteria del Sostengo

Via delle Colonnelle, 5 — 5 min from the Pantheon

Amazingly simple truffle pasta — just pasta, olive oil, and piles of black truffle. Very small inside, even the patio. Reservations recommended.

Map ↗

Poldo e Gianna Osteria

Vicolo Rosini, 6

Delightful dinner — 1 Michelin star tasting menu. Ideally sit outdoors; ground floor is better than the basement.

Map ↗

Piccolo Arancio

Vicolo Scanderbeg, 112

Lovely little place with great fresh pasta and traditional meat dishes. Will need a reservation.

Map ↗

Da Cicero

Via Poli, 44

Good pizza, though more flatbread-style than traditional Italian.

Map ↗

Alfredo alla Scrofa Restaurant

The Alfredo tasting menu, in the original home of Alfredo (as in fettuccine Alfredo) — decor to match. Worth noting: they add a 20% service charge to the bill, which is unusual for Italy.

Map ↗

La Boccaccia

Favorite Roman flatbread-style pizza — pay by weight, they heat it up for you. In Trastevere, about a 20-min walk from St. Peter's.

Map ↗

Bonci

Haven't eaten at the Rome originals, but they're famous for the best Roman flatbread style — they just opened their first US location, in Chicago. It's delicious.

Map ↗

Obicà

Great mozzarella bar — good stop for happy hour, wine, bread, and mozz. A few locations; the Piazza di Firenze one is secluded and less touristy.

Map ↗

Trattoria Da Teo

Never got in — sold out both times without a reservation. If you'll be in Trastevere, book ahead.

Map ↗

Il Marchese – Osteria Mercato Liquori

A stop on our carbonara tour — beautiful interior space, but too trendy and the food wasn't great. Pasta served in skillets.

Map ↗

Trieste Pizza

Adorable little pizzas. Pretty mediocre, honestly — wouldn't go out of your way for it.

Map ↗

La Prosciutteria Navona (now closed)

Great pork sandwiches — stumbled onto it while walking, just down the street from Frigidarium.

Map ↗
Gelato⌄

Old Bridge Gelateria

Viale dei Bastioni di Michelangelo — 5 min walk from St. Peter's

Favorite gelato in Rome. Get the Fragola, Pistacchio, and Stracciatella.

Map ↗

Frigidarium

Via del Governo Vecchio, 112 — 5 min walk from Piazza Navona

Second favorite gelato in Rome — try the chocolate shell!

Map ↗

Grom

Decent gelato chain, lots of locations — this one just north of Piazza Navona.

Map ↗

Gelateria Giolitti

Very old and very famous gelato shop & bakery. A bit overpriced, honestly not much better than the Grom a block away — unless you're sitting for a coffee and pastry.

Things to See & Do⌄

Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine Hill

One ticket, do them together. Book online in advance — it's ruined enough to still be worth it, and they've reopened the underground and 4th ring after decades closed.

Colosseum ↗ Forum ↗ Palatine ↗

Vatican Museums + St. Peter's

Go at night if you can (book in advance) — far fewer crowds, and the museum is beautifully lit. Guided tour recommended for a first visit. Climb the dome once — that's plenty.

Vatican Museums ↗ St. Peter's ↗

The Pantheon

Largest freestanding masonry dome in the world — breathtaking. Now requires tickets, and they're nearly impossible to buy online in advance; expect a kiosk line or a marked-up tour ticket. Worth figuring out — this is a top-5 site, honestly skip the Colosseum over this if you have to choose.

Map ↗

Trevi Fountain

Amazing at night. During the day you now have to pay to get down in front and throw your coin — go in the evening and skip the fee.

Map ↗

Piazza Navona

Home to Bernini's Four Rivers Fountain. Awesome Christmas market in winter, amazing plaza the rest of the year.

Map ↗

Campo de' Fiori

Fun market during the day; lots of tourist bars and restaurants (see La Carbonara above) at night.

Map ↗

Borghese Gallery and Museum

Good Renaissance art gallery in the park that was formerly the Villa's grounds. Book a guided tour — we had a great semi-private one.

Map ↗ Book tour ↗

Castel Sant'Angelo

Finally did a tour here — great, learned a lot. The papal apartments on the roof were surprisingly well preserved. Ours ended up private (no one else signed up) with a knowledgeable guide.

Map ↗ Book tour ↗

Capitoline Museum

Great museum of Roman history — home to the giant foot and face of Nero you see on postcards.

Map ↗

Domus Aurea

Saved for a future visit — one of the great ruins (Nero's Villa), mentioned on our Colosseum & Roman Forum tour. Book ahead.

Map ↗

Walking tours — Context Travel

Led by historians or archeologists — more expensive, smaller groups, higher quality. We did "Underground Rome" and "Caesar and Empire."

Context Travel ↗ Underground Rome ↗ Caesar & Empire ↗

Ostia Antica

Ancient former port town about an hour outside historical Rome — incredible ruins, similar vibe to Pompeii but almost empty. We finally did this with a guided day-trip tour: train out, a few hours walking the site with a guide. Worth doing guided — there's a lot you'd walk right past alone.

Tour ↗
Churches⌄

All of the churches are amazing, and they're (almost) all free to go into (which is unusual in Europe). So long as it isn't mass, just wander into them. And most of them are in piazzas, so there's probably a place to stop and have wine and/or gelato before and/or after.

Chiesa del Gesù

Unassuming on the exterior — an amazing 3D ceiling inside. A short walk from the Pantheon.

Map ↗

Santa Maria della Concezione dei Cappuccini

Sits on top of an amazing crypt made of the bones of former friars. Only a detour if you're staying near Via Vittorio Veneto.

Map ↗

Basilica di Santa Maria in Ara Coeli

Off to the left of the altar is a room with the baby Christ figure — interesting to compare the posted story to the Wikipedia version. Right behind the "wedding cake" and Capitoline Museum.

Map ↗

Angels & Demons Churches

Fire — Santa Maria della Vittoria. Earth — Santa Maria del Popolo. Wind — St. Peter's Basilica. Water — Sant'Agnese in Agone.

Fire ↗ Earth ↗ Wind ↗ Water ↗
Hotels & Places to Stay⌄

When we go for a full week, we'll often rent an apartment instead.

The Pantheon Iconic Rome Hotel, Autograph Collection

Marriott property right next to the Pantheon — amazing location. Hard to get a good rate or use points. Fun rooftop bar (no view of the Pantheon, but lovely nonetheless).

Marriott ↗ Map ↗

Boscolo Exedra

Former Marriott property, hard to get redemptions but a beautiful hotel — closest Marriott property to Termini train station.

Map ↗

Rome Marriott Grand Hotel Flora

A bit of a hike up the hill from the train station. Excellent lounge on the roof for breakfast.

Map ↗

The Westin Excelsior, Rome

Stayed here in 2026, upgraded to a huge room — 20 ft ceilings, a terrace, and a keyhole view of St. Peter's. It's an old hotel and the room was dated, but for the right price we'd go back. Extensive breakfast buffet.
